ITZY "Wannabe" Dance Tutorial: Step-by-Step Breakdown for Self-Learners

"Wannabe" is one of the most popular ITZY choreographies to cover, and for good reason — it's energetic, satisfying to execute, and has a confident attitude that translates well to individual performance. It's also approachable: the choreography has a lot of repetition, manageable tempo, and clear visual reference points. Here's how to break it down systematically.

Overall Structure

"Wannabe" follows a standard K-pop structure: intro, verse 1, pre-chorus, chorus, verse 2, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge, final chorus. The most iconic section — and the one most covers focus on — is the chorus. If your goal is to post a cover, learning the full chorus plus the pre-chorus that leads into it gives you the most complete-looking result for the effort invested.

The Chorus: What to Focus On

The signature chorus move of "Wannabe" is a confident side-to-side weight shift with arm movements that emphasize the chest and shoulders. The footwork is relatively simple — a step-touch pattern — which lets you focus energy on making the upper body look powerful. Common mistake: dancers over-execute the footwork and under-execute the arm and chest movement. The song's attitude comes from the upper body, not the feet. Practice the arm pattern sitting down first, without footwork, until it feels natural, then add the feet.

The "I wanna be me" phrase has a specific hand gesture that covers tend to either execute perfectly or miss completely — it requires a deliberate wrist flick at the right moment. Slow the reference video to 0.5x speed and count exactly when the wrist action occurs relative to the lyrics. Then drill it in isolation 20 times before recombining with surrounding movements.

How Long It Takes

With focused practice of 45 minutes per session, 4 sessions per week, a dedicated beginner can have the chorus to a filmable level in 2–3 weeks. The full song (minus the more complex bridge) is achievable in 5–7 weeks.
