BTS "Dynamite" Dance Tutorial: The Most Beginner-Friendly BTS Choreo

Among BTS's extensive catalog of choreography, "Dynamite" stands out as one of the most accessible for self-learners. It's upbeat, retro-flavored, and built around moves that are fun to do even if you're just starting out. The full choreography runs about 3.5 minutes, but even learning the chorus section gives you something satisfying to perform.

Dynamite was released during a period when BTS was performing more on international stages, and the choreography reflects that — it's designed to be visually clear from a distance, with big, clean shapes rather than intricate close-up details. This makes it easier to learn by video because the moves read clearly even on a small screen.

Shoulder and Upper Body Work

Dynamite is heavily shoulder-focused. Several sections feature alternating shoulder pops and rolls that give the choreo its groovy, funky character. If you're not used to shoulder isolations, start by practicing "shoulder pops" in isolation: stand relaxed, then sharply push one shoulder forward while pulling the other back. Do this to a slow beat (60 BPM) until it feels natural, then speed up. In the actual choreography, these shoulder movements are combined with arm positions that need to happen simultaneously — so getting the shoulders automatic first will free your attention for the arms.

The Chorus Step Pattern

The chorus has a traveling step-touch with a heel dig that's immediately recognizable. Step right, touch left heel forward, step left, touch right heel forward — all while doing the arm choreography. Practice the feet alone first to a simple count, then add arms. The "Shining through the city with a little funk and soul" section of the chorus has the most compact combination of moves and is worth drilling in slow motion before attempting at full speed.

Making It Look Natural

The single biggest quality difference between beginners and experienced dancers performing Dynamite is naturalness. Beginners execute moves sequentially, pausing slightly between each one. Experienced dancers have internalized the transitions so deeply that moves blend together. The solution isn't talent — it's repetition. Once you know the moves, drill short 4-beat phrases 50 times each. Your body needs to stop thinking and start feeling.
