BLACKPINK Dance Tutorial: Breaking Down Their Iconic Moves

By Wujing Team · August 2025 · 8 min read

BLACKPINK's choreography has a quality that separates it from most K-pop groups: it looks dangerous. Their movements have weight, intention, and an edge that communicates power rather than cuteness. Choreographer Cheshir Ha built their signature style around what YG Entertainment calls "girl crush" energy — a blend of hip-hop attitude, precise isolation work, and theatrical confidence. This breakdown covers the technical elements you need to understand before attempting any BLACKPINK piece, then dives into specific iconic moments you can start practicing today.

Understanding the BLACKPINK Movement Philosophy

Before touching specific choreography, you need to understand one principle that governs all BLACKPINK movement: every move ends before the next one begins. Unlike groups that favor flowing, connected transitions (TWICE's style, for example), BLACKPINK choreography uses hard stops — sharp endings that create visual punctuation. When a BLACKPINK arm move lands, it locks. The next move begins fresh rather than flowing from the previous one.

This locking quality comes from something called isolation: moving one body part while keeping everything else completely still. When Jennie's arm slices across her body, her shoulders don't move. When Lisa drops her hip, her upper body stays level. Learning to isolate takes deliberate practice, but it's the single skill that will most quickly make your BLACKPINK covers look authentic.

The Four Signature Movement Elements

1. Sharp Arm Isolations

BLACKPINK's arm work is characterized by straight lines and sudden direction changes. The arm doesn't arc — it moves in one direction, stops completely, then moves in a new direction. To practice this, extend your right arm straight out to the side at shoulder height. Without moving your shoulder, bend only at the elbow so your forearm points upward. Hold for one beat. Then extend the forearm back out. That "forearm flick" is the fundamental unit of BLACKPINK arm vocabulary.

Common errors: the shoulder creeping up, the elbow bending before the arm reaches full extension, and rushing through the hold. The hold — even if it's just one count — is what makes the isolation visible.

2. The Hip Drop

This is one of BLACKPINK's most copied and most incorrectly executed moves. A hip drop is not a hip circle — it's a downward isolation. Stand with feet slightly wider than hip-width. Without bending your knees, let your right hip drop straight down by releasing the right side of your waist. Your weight stays centered. Then lift back to neutral. The common mistake is shifting your entire weight to one side, which creates a side-sway rather than a drop. Practice in front of a mirror with your shoulders staying completely level. If your shoulder drops when your hip drops, you're shifting weight instead of isolating.

3. The Attitude Walk

BLACKPINK's transitions between formations often use a particular walk — weight forward over the ball of the foot, slight lean in the torso, gaze steady and directed. It's not a saunter and it's not a march. Practice this by walking with 90% of your weight on your front foot as each step lands. Let your arms hang with soft tension (not swinging, not stiff). The neck stays long. This walk is how the group maintains "character" between the big choreography moments.

4. Simultaneous Floor and Air Levels

Many BLACKPINK formations feature members at different heights — one standing, one in a deep plie, one on the floor. Even if you're practicing solo, knowing this spatial logic helps you understand why certain moves have a particular body height. Moves that look like they end in a neutral position are often designed to contrast with a member who's much lower or higher beside you.

Kill This Love: The Cross Arm Section

The most-learned BLACKPINK sequence is probably the "Kill This Love" chorus, specifically the crossed-arm chest sequence at "kill this love." Here's the count breakdown:

  1. Count 1: Both arms cross in front of chest, right arm on top, sharp stop
  2. Count 2: Hold (this hold is critical — don't rush to 3)
  3. Count 3: Both arms uncross and extend outward at shoulder height
  4. Count 4: Arms drop to sides, head drops with them
  5. Count 5–6: Head and gaze lift back up (the "resurrection" beat)
  6. Counts 7–8: Prepare position for next phrase

The common mistake is treating counts 1 and 3 as equal-energy moments. Count 1 (the cross) is maximum power. Count 3 (the open) is release — slightly less force. The dynamic difference is what makes it dramatic rather than robotic.

How You Like That: The Hair Flip Sequence

The "How You Like That" comeback performance introduced a hair flip sequence that became one of the most-covered K-pop moments of 2020. The move works like this: the head tilts forward on count 7 of the previous phrase, then whips back sharply on count 1. The hair follows the head due to momentum — it's not a separate "hair flip," it's a consequence of the head movement. If you have short hair, the same head movement still reads correctly; the hair flip is visual bonus, not the actual technique.

After the head whip, the gaze locks immediately. That's the power move. Dancers who look around after the flip dissipate the impact entirely.

Pink Venom: The Weapon Stance

Pink Venom's choreography opens with what fans call the "weapon stance" — feet at 45-degree angles, knees slightly bent, arms positioned as though holding weapons at each side. The key technical element here is the simultaneous knee bend and torso level. When you bend into the stance, your chest stays upright — it doesn't lean forward or back. Think of your torso descending straight down like an elevator, not tilting like a seesaw. This is what makes the stance look powerful instead of awkward.

Practice Tips for Non-Dancers

If you're coming to BLACKPINK choreography with no dance background, here's your priority order:

  • Spend the first two weeks exclusively on isolation drills — hip drops, arm isolations, shoulder pops — before touching any choreography
  • Record your isolation practice and compare to reference videos. Isolations look very different from inside your body versus on camera
  • Learn the footwork pattern of any section first, then add arms. BLACKPINK footwork is often simpler than it looks — the arms carry the visual complexity
  • Don't skip the "girl crush" face. Blank expression turns powerful choreography into a gymnastics routine. Practice your look in the mirror separately from your body movement
  • Start at 50% speed with YouTube's speed control. BLACKPINK's tempo feels manageable at half speed and reveals all the micro-movements you'd otherwise miss

BLACKPINK choreography rewards patience and precision over speed. You can learn the shape of "Kill This Love" in an afternoon. You'll spend weeks making it actually sharp. That's not a flaw in your learning — it's the nature of isolation-based technique, which takes longer to feel natural than groove-based movement. Once it clicks, though, your whole body's movement quality changes.
