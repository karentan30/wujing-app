How to Set Up the Perfect Home Dance Practice Space

The most common excuse for not practicing dance is "I don't have a studio." The truth is, most choreography — including K-pop and Chinese traditional dance — can be practiced in a small cleared space. You don't need much. What you do need is the right setup for safe, effective practice and, crucially, for filming yourself so you can improve.

Minimum Space Requirements

For most choreography, you need a clear rectangle of about 2 meters wide by 2 meters deep. This is roughly the size of a large yoga mat plus half again on each side. Move furniture, roll up rugs, and clear any tripping hazards. Flooring matters: hardwood or laminate is ideal because it lets you pivot and slide. Thick carpet grips your feet unexpectedly and can cause ankle injuries on turns. If you only have carpet, wear dance shoes or smooth-soled socks.

Filming Position

Set your phone at chest-to-head height, angled slightly downward, about 2–2.5 meters in front of your practice space. This gives you a full-body view without excessive distortion. Avoid filming from floor level (makes your feet large and your head tiny) or from too high (makes arm extensions look flat). Natural light from a window in front of you (not behind you) is the simplest good lighting setup. If natural light isn't available, a single bright light source in front of you is better than multiple ambient lights that create shadows.

Sound Setup

Practice with a speaker rather than phone speakers or headphones. You need to hear the music spatially — not just in one ear — to feel the rhythm in your body properly. Headphones create a dissociated experience where the music is "inside your head" while your body is in the room, which subtly disrupts your physical connection to the beat. Even a cheap Bluetooth speaker pointed at you is significantly better than earbuds for dance practice.

Building a Practice Routine

Structure your sessions: 10 minutes of warm-up (joint circles, light cardio), 30–40 minutes of focused choreography work, 5 minutes of cool-down stretching. Don't practice dance for more than 60–75 minutes in a single session without a meaningful break — after this point, fatigue causes you to repeat mistakes rather than correct them, which reinforces the wrong patterns. Shorter, more frequent sessions (4–5 times per week of 45 minutes) outperform marathon weekend sessions for skill acquisition.
