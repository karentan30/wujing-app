NewJeans "Hype Boy" Dance Tutorial: Breaking Down the Iconic Moves

NewJeans' "Hype Boy" is one of the most rewatched choreographies of the past few years — and for good reason. The choreography is deceptively simple on first glance but packed with precise timing and subtle weight shifts that separate a clean execution from a sloppy one. Whether you're learning it for a cover, a class, or just for fun, here's how to approach it systematically.

The choreography was created for a five-member group, so solo learners need to decide which member's part to follow. Minji's center parts are the most commonly learned and are well documented in the official practice video.

The Signature "Bounce" Quality

Hype Boy has a distinctive bouncy, youthful energy that comes from consistently bending the knees slightly and letting the body respond to each beat with a natural up-down motion. This isn't just about the arms or the specific gestures — it's a full-body quality that needs to be present from the first count. Practice standing in a slight plié and bouncing gently to the track before you even try the moves. Get that baseline quality in your body first.

The pre-chorus section ("Boy, you got me...") uses a rolling arm motion combined with a lateral step-touch. The key detail most learners miss: the arms don't just move horizontally — they travel in a slight figure-eight path that gives it the flowing, almost casual quality. Film yourself from the front and check that your arms have this arc, not a straight push-pull.

Footwork: The Shuffle Sequence

The chorus footwork involves a quick shuffle pattern — a triple step that many dancers default to a simpler two-step. Listen for the triplet subdivision in the rhythm and match your footwork to it. Left-right-left, then right-left-right, staying light on the balls of your feet. Practice this footwork in isolation to a metronome before adding arm layers.

Using Slow Motion to Nail the Details

The bridge section of Hype Boy has the most complex arm isolations in the piece. Slow the reference video to 25% and watch just the wrists. You'll notice small wrist flicks on the off-beats that you'd completely miss at full speed. These micro-movements are what make the performance look polished versus robotic. AI dance analysis tools can detect these frame-level differences and show you where your angles diverge from the reference — saving hours of guessing.
